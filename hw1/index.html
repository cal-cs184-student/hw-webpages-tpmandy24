Task 1: Drawing Single-Color Triangles

We went through the process of building the rasterizer through the smallest value of our x and y coordinates. If it fits within the range of our constraints we then can rasterize the triangle and fill it in with the given color as in instructed. The process of rasterization is when a smooth graphic or image is converted into a series of pixels. These pixels can present itself as singlar pixels, lines, dots, and for this specific instance triangles. In other words, a graphic is converted and utilized as a series of pixels to create a raster image. Our algorithm is no worse than the one that checks each sample within the boundaries of the defined triangles that are displayed wihtin the smallest rectangle. We minimize the binding box of the triangle then enforce edge cases and checks to make sure it stays within bounds. This then efficiently correlates to each prospective pixel.

Task 2: Antialiasing by Supersampling

At a sample rate of 1, each pixel is evaluated only once at its center, resulting in binary coverage and visibly jagged edges along thin triangles. When the sample rate is increased to 4, each pixel is subdivided into a 2×2 grid of sub-samples, allowing partial triangle coverage to be captured and producing smoother edges. At a sample rate of 16, a 4×4 grid is used, which further refines edge coverage and significantly reduces aliasing artifacts. These results occur because supersampling approximates the fraction of a pixel covered by the triangle by averaging multiple subpixel samples.
